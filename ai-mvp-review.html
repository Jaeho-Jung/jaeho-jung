Who this is for

Founders, small product teams, and agencies that have built an AI product quickly and need an independent check before launch, client delivery, or the next round of engineering work.

This is most useful when the product already has a working build, but the team still needs to know which claims are supported by observed behavior and which are only demonstrated by a happy path.

What I do

The review follows one simple chain: Claim → Test → Evidence → Severity → Recommendation.

I focus on observable product behavior: whether the core flow works under the conditions you claim, where it fails, whether failure states are handled, and what evidence is strong enough to support a release decision.

You receive

  • Evidence Matrix: claim or flow, result, evidence, severity, recommendation
  • Top 5 launch risks: the issues most likely to matter to users or delivery
  • Fix now / Fix later: prioritized rather than undifferentiated bug lists
  • Tested / Not tested boundary: exactly what the review does and does not establish

What this looks like in practice

TetoEgen

Consumer AI product

I built and evaluated a deployed multi-vertical AI product where different claims required different evidence: face analysis, image classification, geometry-driven hairstyle recommendation, and deterministic questionnaire scoring. The case study shows how input validity, dataset integrity, inspectable recommendation logic, and failure boundaries were separated instead of being hidden behind one generic “AI” layer.

LetsPlay Infant Speech ASR

Speech AI · deployment evidence

In a real-time child-speech project, model conversion succeeded while runtime behavior failed, synthetic data looked stronger than the target-domain evidence, and higher server throughput produced unacceptable tail latency. Those findings changed the architecture and removed benchmark claims the measurement method could not support.
